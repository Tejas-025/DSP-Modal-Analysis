Engineering Theory, DSP Methodology & Reviewer Verification Notes

1. Euler–Bernoulli Beam Theory, Spatial Coupling φₙ(x_s) & Slenderness

For a uniform rectangular cross-section (A = b·h, I = b·h³/12), the undamped and damped modal frequencies are:

fₙ = (βₙ² / 2πL²) · √(E·I / ρ·A) ,     f_d,n = fₙ · √(1 - ζₙ²)

Each mode's contribution at sensor position x_s is weighted by the unity-normalized spatial eigenfunction φₙ(x_s/L). When h/L > 0.10, an engineering warning is raised because Timoshenko shear deformation and rotary inertia become significant.

2. Impulse Initial Conditions, Windowing & Unbiased Mode-1 Damping Identification

An ideal impulse excitation imparts initial velocity at zero initial displacement (x(0) = 0, φₙ = 0):

x(t) = Σ [ Aₙ · φₙ(x_s) · e^(-ζₙ ωₙ t) · sin(ω_d,n t) ] + η(t)

Because an impulse decay has its highest signal-to-noise ratio at t = 0, applying a tapered Hann window zeroes out the strongest signal energy. Thus, Rectangular or Exponential windows are recommended for transient ringdowns. Damping ζ̂₁ is estimated with zero ground-truth blending by isolating Mode 1 via a narrow zero-phase bandpass filter ([0.65·f_d,1, 1.40·f_d,1]) and fitting a linear regression to ln(peak) vs. t.

3. Prewarped Butterworth filtfilt Cascade & Exact Bluestein FFT

A prototype N-th order Butterworth filter consists of N/2 biquad sections with distinct pole Q-factors Q_k = 1 / [2·sin((2k-1)π / 2N)]. Running the cascade forward and backward (filtfilt) doubles the effective filter order and squares the transfer function magnitude. To place the true -3 dB point after filtfilt exactly at f_c, the prototype cutoff is prewarped by (√2 - 1)^(-1 / 2N).
